
Playbook: storyboarding

Use when the brief is a script, scenario or shot list for film, animation, games or advertising. The deliverable is a sequence of readable panels, not finished illustrations: clarity of staging, continuity and timing come before rendering.

Plan before drawing. Read the scenario and list its scenes (one location and time each) and, inside each scene, its beats: every change of action, emotion, speaker or camera. One beat is usually one panel; a camera move or a large action can need a start and an end panel. Decide a shot for each beat: establish a new location with a wide or extreme-wide shot, move closer for emotion and detail (medium, medium-close, close-up), and use inserts for objects that matter. Vary size and angle with purpose; low angles add power, high angles vulnerability, Dutch angles unease. Keep the 180° line within a scene: characters keep their screen side and look in a consistent direction unless a panel shows the camera crossing.

Build the outline with the storyboard tools before drawing. Create one scene per add_storyboard_panels call, with Slugging such as "INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT" on its first panel, Action describing what happens in present tense, Dialogue as NAME: line, and Notes for camera and sound (PAN LEFT, PUSH IN, SFX: door slam). Give each panel the time its action or line needs: roughly 2–3 seconds for a simple beat, about one second per three words of dialogue plus a beat, shorter for fast action. Check total_seconds against any target length.

Break a screenplay down yourself when the person asks for a breakdown rather than a mechanical import (import_storyboard_script splits one panel per paragraph or line). Work scene by scene: read_storyboard_script gives scenes (s1, s2…) with their beats, stable beat IDs (s1b3), speaking characters, word counts and estimated seconds; long scripts come in pages, so call it again with from_scene set to next_scene. For each scene decide the coverage: open a new location or time with an establishing wide (or extreme wide) shot carrying the Slugging; give each dialogue exchange a panel, as a two-shot or over-the-shoulder for the exchange and a close-up for the line that turns the scene, rather than one panel per sentence; give key action its own panel and an insert for the object that matters (a key, a phone screen, a handle turning); add a reaction panel where a character's silence tells the story; and split a long action paragraph wherever the shot changes. Then write the whole breakdown as one build_storyboard_from_breakdown call: scenes named by their heading, panels with captions by field name (Action in present tense, Dialogue as NAME: line, Slugging on the scene's first panel), shot notes in notes, the camera move in camera (PUSH IN, PAN LEFT, HANDHELD), size and angle, and the source_beats each panel shows. Pass the script path as script so every beat ID is checked, and cover the beats it lists as uncovered_beats or leave them out on purpose. Errors name the scene and panel; fix that entry and send the whole breakdown again, since nothing is added until it is valid. The build is one Undo step.

Estimate lengths from the words, then use judgement. Panels without seconds or frames are timed from their captions when built; estimate_storyboard_durations re-times a scope (panels, scenes or scene_names; default the whole board) by word rate: spoken dialogue at about 150 words a minute plus a pause after each line and each parenthetical, action lines at about 120, never under a minimum. Run it with dry_run first and read old and new seconds per panel and per scene; pass rates (dialogue_wpm, action_wpm, line_pause, parenthetical_pause, minimum_seconds, or the fields counted as dialogue and action) for a fast-talking comedy or a slow drama. Words time speech well but not action: a fight, a chase or a reveal needs longer than its sentence, a reaction or a held look needs a beat with no words, and an insert can be very short. After applying the estimate, set those panels with set_storyboard_timing, and fit a scene to a known target with fit_storyboard_timing. Locked panels keep their duration.

Draw rough and readable. On each panel keep separate named layers: Background, then one layer per character or prop, then Effects/Arrows. Draw a clear silhouette and the key pose, simple perspective for the set (a horizon line, a vanishing point), and use value only to separate planes. Movement arrows: a curved arrow on its own layer shows character motion; a framed arrow or a second rectangle shows camera moves.

Use vector strokes for line work that must stay editable. add_vector_layer makes a vector stroke layer; draw_vector_strokes draws centreline strokes whose points carry a width (pressure, multiplying line_width) and an opacity, so ramping width from about 0.1 to 1 and back gives a tapered pencil line, and fading opacity gives a soft start. Clean outlines, panel props, speed lines and camera arrows suit vectors: after review, retouch_vector_strokes thickens or thins line weight along a path, edit_vector_strokes smooths, simplifies, recolours or moves strokes, and erase_vector_strokes trims overshoots, all without redrawing. draw_vector_shapes adds lines, rectangles, ellipses and polylines as editable strokes. Keep rough tone, texture and value on bitmap layers with paint and the board's brushes; describe_vector_strokes reads strokes back by index.

Continue shots instead of redrawing them. When the next panel keeps the same set-up, duplicate_project_page the previous panel, then translate_node or set_transform the character layers, repaint only the pose that changes, and update its captions with update_storyboard_panel. To bring a character into a different shot, copy_page_nodes from the panel where it was drawn and rescale or repaint it there; without dx/dy the copy lands in place, at the same position in the frame, so a character or prop that stays put needs no re-aligning. Inspect each finished scene with get_view on its panels and describe_storyboard, then fix staging, continuity or timing before moving on.

Draw for the camera frame. describe_storyboard lists the Stage `guides` with their rectangles in panel pixels: keep faces, key action and any on-screen text inside `title_safe_rect`, everything that must read inside `action_safe_rect`, and use `field_rects` (turn the field guide on with set_storyboard_settings guides) to plan a push-in or truck to a smaller field; `stage_area` is the overscan around the frame for art a pan will reveal. Rough in the board `palette` greys (light for construction, darker for the final line) and put notes, corrections and camera arrows on their own layer in the palette's red or blue so they read as notes, not art. Add a project colour with set_storyboard_settings palette add. Paint uses the board's brushes (list_brushes; import_brushes brings in ABR (.abr) brushes), mirror or symmetry for symmetric props and sets, and set_blend_mode, set_clip and add_mask for shading on clipped layers.

Start from art the person already has. import_storyboard_files with absolute paths of PSD, ORA, PNG or JPEG files makes one panel per file (a layout per shot, named after the file) after a panel, or with into "layers" places the files on top of a panel as reference or a background plate. Pictures are cropped to the centre and fitted to the frame; PSD groups, blend modes and clipping are kept, and the result lists them. Lower a reference layer's opacity and draw on a new layer above it. For a reference image the person attached in the chat, look at it with get_reference_image and draw from it.

Reuse recurring characters, props and sets through the storyboard library instead of redrawing them: add_to_storyboard_library saves chosen layers (or a whole panel), list_storyboard_library shows the project and personal items, and place_storyboard_library_item puts layers on the active panel at their original position or a panel item in as a new panel.

Rough a sequence on thumbnail sheets first when the staging is still open. set_storyboard_thumbnail_sheet turns a panel into a grid of small camera frames and returns each cell's rectangle; draw one quick thumbnail per cell with the drawing tools, compare the flow on one page, then convert_storyboard_thumbnails turns the sheet into one panel per cell, in order, each scaled to full size with its layers still editable. Add captions and timing to the converted panels afterwards.

Keep the set with Smart add. Put the set or background on a layer named in the Smart add list (set_storyboard_settings smart_add_layers, "Background" by default); smart_add_storyboard_panel then starts the next panel with a copy of that layer and the same shot size, so only the characters need drawing.

Edit the board without losing work. move_storyboard_panels reorders panels and moves them between scenes; start_storyboard_group splits a scene and join_storyboard_group merges it back into the previous one; copy_storyboard_panels repeats panels or whole scenes, and import_storyboard_panels brings scenes in from another storyboard file. Lock panels and scenes the user has approved with set_storyboard_locks so later edits cannot touch them. After inserting, moving or deleting panels, renumber_storyboard renames panels (and scenes, if the board uses numbered scenes) by the naming rules set with set_storyboard_settings naming. When a character is renamed, replace_in_storyboard_captions with match_case for each spelling (Mia, MIA) and whole_word, after checking the matches with find_in_storyboard_captions; it reports locked panels it skipped. Use add_storyboard_caption_field for extra fields (Camera, Sound, VFX), and format_storyboard_caption to bold a character's first appearance or colour a sound cue.

Time the animatic to its sound. describe_storyboard gives each panel's `start` and `timecode`, and `animatic` the running time, the audio tracks and the sound library with each sound's duration. Read the dialogue and sound lengths first: a line's panel lasts at least as long as the line plus a beat to react. Put the dialogue on its own track (import_storyboard_sound, add_storyboard_audio_track, place_storyboard_sound at_panel or at_timecode), add_storyboard_markers on it at each line, hit and musical beat, then time panels to them with set_storyboard_timing and snap_storyboard_cuts so cuts land on the beats; roll_storyboard_cut nudges one cut without changing the total. fit_storyboard_timing scales a scene or a selection to a target length, keeping the rhythm, such as a scene to its dialogue or a sequence to a spot length. Cut by default. Use set_storyboard_transitions sparingly and keep them short (a few frames to a second): a dissolve for time passing or a dream, a wipe or slide for a stylised change of place, and fade_to_color black where a sequence ends and the next begins. Locked panels refuse timing changes, so time panels before locking them.

Animate the board where motion tells the story better than more panels. Each scene has one camera keyed from the scene start (describe_storyboard_camera lists its panels' starts and the rest framing: the frame centre, zoom 1), so a pan or truck that follows a character across several panels is two or three set_storyboard_camera_keys keys on the scene, given at a frame, seconds or timecode, or from a `panel`'s start; x/y are the shot centre in panel pixels and zoom 2 shows half the frame. Ease camera moves in and out (ease_in_out) so they start and stop naturally; linear suits a steady pan and step a hold and cut. Draw the art wider than the frame (into the overscan `stage_area`) where a pan will reveal it, and keep the action inside the camera frame at every key. Add set_storyboard_camera_shake (earthquake or bumpy_ride for a hit or explosion, handheld for tension) only where something shakes the shot, and set_storyboard_static_camera to hold one panel still inside a moving scene. For a simple slide, turn or fade of one character within a panel, put keys on its layer with set_storyboard_layer_keys (x/y offsets in px, rotation about a pivot set with set_storyboard_layer_pivot, opacity 0–1) instead of drawing many next-frame panels; keep separate layers for what moves. Save alternate looks of a panel (lights on or off, a prop shown or not) as layer comps with capture_storyboard_layer_comp and switch them with apply_storyboard_layer_comp. Add an animated scene to the library with add_to_storyboard_library `scene` to reuse it whole.

Block shots in 3D with the Shot Generator when staging, lens and angle matter more than drawing. Each panel can hold a 3D set (metres, Y up, ground at y = 0, yaw 0 faces the default camera): text_to_storyboard_shot builds one from a phrase such as "low-angle close-up of two people at a table" and lists the words it did not understand; then refine it yourself rather than settling for the offline parser. add_storyboard_shot_object places characters (adult_male, adult_female, adult_neutral, child), props (table, chair, door, wall, car, tree, stairs…) and key, fill or rim lights; pose_storyboard_character sets a pose preset (sit, walk, point, fight_stance…), joint angles, hand shapes, a face, IK targets that put a hand on a doorknob or a foot on a step, and a look_at point that keeps eyelines consistent across the scene; set_storyboard_shot_camera frames a subject by shot size, angle and side with a lens (wide 18–24 mm for space, 35–50 mm for coverage, 85–135 mm for close-ups). For a complex set, read describe_storyboard_shot, edit its `set` JSON and send it whole with set_storyboard_shot. explore_storyboard_shots proposes angles to choose from. Keep continuity by duplicating a panel (its set comes with it) and moving only the camera. render_storyboard_shot mode reference puts the set in a locked, half-transparent "Shot Generator" layer (outline style for tracing) that re-renders when the set changes; draw the panel on layers above it, or use mode snapshot for a layer to paint over. Give layers depth with set_storyboard_layer_depth so camera moves show parallax, and attach_storyboard_layer makes a drawn layer follow a character or prop when the set changes; with `at` (a panel pixel) it lays a pixel layer on the surface there (a sign on a wall, a decal on a car), warped to the surface's angle.

Worked plan: field guide and a notes colour, a scene with a tapered vector line under the character, its next frame and a Smart add panel, the character pasted in place into the close-up, a slow eased truck across the kitchen scene and Mia sliding into her next frame, a thumbnail sheet for the next scene, then a character rename, a lock and panel renumbering, a dissolve into the street and the street scene fitted to four seconds, and finally a hallway scene built from a breakdown, its lengths estimated as a dry run and the insert held a little longer by judgement.
