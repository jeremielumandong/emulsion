# Storyboard 3D and Shot Generator

Phase 11 adds 3D sets to the Storyboard workspace, in the spirit of
Storyboarder's Shot Generator: place posable mannequins and props, choose a
lens, shot size and angle, light the set, and render a toon reference to draw
over. The first part of this guide is the Shot Generator view in the
Storyboard workspace; the rest describes the engine in the `emulsion-scene`
crate, which the view, the reference layers, exports and MCP tools all
share.

Everything here works offline. The mannequins and props are generated by
Emulsion itself; no third-party models are bundled.

## The Shot Generator view

Every panel can hold its own 3D set. Open it on the active panel with the
**3D** button on the Stage toolbar, **Shot Generator** in the panel
inspector, **View › Shot Generator** or **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+G**; it takes the
Stage's place until **Done** (or the same shortcut). It shows the active
panel's set and follows when another panel becomes active.

### Viewport

The viewport renders the set off the UI thread at a reduced resolution while
you drag (lower still when a frame takes longer than about 30 ms, so drags
stay smooth in large windows) and sharp again, at the display's resolution,
when you stop; frames that are out of date when they finish are dropped, so
the interface never waits for a render. Moving the camera reuses the
prepared set; only edits to the set prepare it again.

- **Camera** looks through the shot camera, framed like the panel. **Free**
  looks around without touching the shot; **Use this view as the shot**
  copies it. **Top** and **Side** are orthographic views for placing objects.
- Right (or middle) drag orbits, Shift+right drag pans and the wheel dollies.
  In Camera view these move the shot camera (one Undo step per drag; the
  wheel commits once it rests); in Top and Side views they pan and zoom.
- Click an object to select it; its outline turns blue. A left drag then
  does what the tool says: **Move** slides it over the ground, **Rotate**
  turns it, **Scale** grows or shrinks it.
- **Gizmos.** The selected object shows the tool's handles, in red (X),
  green (Y) and blue (Z); the one under the pointer lights up yellow.
  **Move** has an arrow per axis and a square per pair of axes: drag an
  arrow to slide along that axis only, a square to slide in that plane.
  **Rotate** has a ring per axis: drag along a ring to turn about its axis.
  **Scale** has a handle per axis to stretch along it and a square in the
  middle to scale evenly (dragging right or up grows). Hold **Shift** while
  dragging to snap: 10 cm, 15° or 10% steps. The **World** / **Local**
  switch on the toolbar (or **X**) lines Move and Rotate up with the world
  or with the object; Scale always uses the object's own axes. Each drag
  is one Undo step.
- **Keys** (while the viewport has focus, after clicking in it): **W**
  Move, **E** Rotate, **R** Scale, **X** World/Local.
- **Pose** shows the selected character's joints. Drag a joint to turn it
  (sideways twists or turns, up and down bends, Shift+sideways swings it out).
  With **IK** on, dragging a hand or foot moves it to a point and the elbow
  or knee follows.

### Side panels

- **Add:** the four mannequins (adult male, adult female, adult, child), the
  built-in props and key, fill and rim lights, placed in front of the camera
  facing it, and **Import model…** (below).
- **Objects:** every object of the set; click one to select it.
- **Selection:** position (left/right, near/far, height), turn and scale
  sliders, colour, Duplicate, Hide, **Casts shadow** (on a key light:
  whether that light casts shadows) and Delete. Characters add the body type
  and sliders (height, build, head, legs, arms, shoulders, hips), the pose
  library and the project's custom poses, **Mirror**, **Bake IK**, **Clear
  IK**, **Save pose** (kept in the project's library), hand shapes for each
  hand, faces and **Look at**: pick a point in the viewport, look at the
  camera or look ahead. The joint picked with the Pose tool gets bend, twist
  and swing sliders. Props add width, height and depth; imported rigs list
  their joints with X, Y and Z sliders; lights add their kind and intensity.
- **Camera:** lenses from 14 to 200 mm, framing the selection (or the first
  character) by shot size with the chosen angle and side, roll, tilt and
  height.
- **Light and ground:** ground, grid and horizon switches and ambient light.
- **Reference layer:** render style (toon, clay, outline for tracing,
  silhouette), **Shadows** (key lights cast shadows in Toon and Clay, in the
  viewport and the reference layer), the layer's opacity and whether it
  updates with the set, and
  **Lay selected layer on a surface** (see [Layers on surfaces](#layers-on-surfaces)).

Each slider pull, drag or click is one Undo step.

### Describe a shot and the Shot Explorer

Type a shot into **Describe a shot** and press Enter or **Build shot**: the
set is replaced by the one the description builds (see
[Describe a shot](#describe-a-shot)), with what was understood and any
unknown words under the viewport. **Shot Explorer** proposes a dozen camera
setups on the selection, each a thumbnail; click one to make it the shot.

### Into the board

- **Use as reference layer** renders the camera view at the panel's
  resolution into a locked layer named "Shot Generator" just above the
  paper, at the reference opacity (50% by default). Rendering again replaces
  its pixels, and while **Update with the set** is on every set change
  renders it again, in the same Undo step as the change. Draw on layers
  above it. Panels without a set are unaffected.
- **Snapshot to layer** adds the render as an ordinary layer at the top to
  draw or paint over.

Thumbnails, the animatic, prints and exports show the reference layer like
any other layer; hide it, or make it a review layer, to leave it out.

### Import models

**Import model…** adds a glTF (`.glb`, or `.gltf` with embedded buffers) or
OBJ file to the set. The file is stored inside the `.emu` (up to 64 models
and 256 MB per project; importing the same file twice keeps one copy) and
models no set uses any more are dropped with the next set edit (a set
saved in the project library keeps its models). A `.gltf` that keeps its
data in separate files is refused: export it as `.glb`.

Models travel with their sets: copying panels and pasting them into another
storyboard, placing a library panel or scene item (personal library items
keep the model files with them), extracting scenes and merging an extract
back all bring the models the sets use, one copy of each (a model's id is
its content's hash). When they would not fit the receiving project's budget,
the paste, placement or merge is refused with a message and nothing
changes.
Rigged (skinned) glTF models are posed by joint name in the inspector.

### Layer depth and parallax

The panel inspector's **3D set and layer depth** section gives each layer a
depth in multiples of the camera's distance to the panel: 0 is the panel, 1 is twice
as far (it moves half as much when the scene camera pans), 4 suits distant
hills and 30 the sky; negative values bring a layer in front, down to −0.9.
Presets (Near, Panel, Mid, Far, Sky) and − / + buttons set it, one Undo step
each. The 2D scene camera is then read as a 35 mm camera: a pan moves it
sideways and a zoom dollies it, so near layers slide and grow more than far
ones in the animatic, movie exports and on the Stage: whenever the scene
camera has moved at the playhead (in Camera view or not, and live while the
camera tool drags), the Stage draws the panel through the same code as the
player, so it matches the animatic frame for frame. At rest the panel looks
exactly as drawn. **Top** and **Side** diagrams show the layers at their depths, the
panel plane and the camera's field of view at the playhead.

With a set on the panel, the same section makes the selected layer
**follow** an object of the set: when the set or its camera changes, the
layer moves with the object on screen and scales with its distance (locked
layers stay put). Use it for speech balloons, effects or a drawn costume
detail over a mannequin.

### Layers on surfaces

A pixel layer can also lie *on* a surface of the set, at its angle: a sign
on a wall, a poster, a decal on a car. Select the layer, press **Lay on a
surface…** in the inspector (or **Lay selected layer on a surface** in the
Shot Generator) and click the surface in the viewport. The layer is warped
in perspective onto the surface's plane as the shot camera sees it: its
centre stays where the click landed, it keeps its drawn size where it
touches the surface, and it is oriented by the surface (its up as close to
the world's up as the surface allows). From then on it is drawn again from
its flat drawing whenever the set changes: the object moving or turning, a
character's bone it lies on, or the camera. The flat drawing is kept in a
hidden, locked copy just below it, named "*layer* (flat)"; to change the
drawing, edit that copy (the surface layer is redrawn from it with the next
set change) rather than the warped layer. Laying a layer is one Undo step,
and so is each set edit with the layers it moves. Layers with masks, and
layers that are not pixels (rasterize them first), cannot lie on surfaces.
Choosing an object in the **follows** row makes the layer follow a point
again.

### Storage

A panel's set is stored with the panel in `storyboard.json`; models are
`models/{id}.glb|gltf|obj` entries in the package. Files without sets are
unchanged. Opening a project checks every set and model; a damaged or
missing model refuses to open rather than losing the set.

## Sets

A set (a `Scene`) holds objects, one shot camera and an environment.

- **Units and axes.** Metres, Y up, the ground is the plane y = 0. Objects
  face +Z when unrotated, so a character's left hand points toward +X.
- **Objects** are characters, props and lights. Each has a stable id (never
  reused, even after deletion), a name, a transform (position; rotation as
  yaw/pitch/roll in degrees or an exact quaternion; per-axis scale), a
  visibility flag, a casts-shadows switch and a base colour.
- **Environment:** sky and horizon colours, ground colour, ground on/off,
  grid on/off and spacing, horizon line on/off and ambient light.
- **Limits:** 256 objects, 2 million triangles after tessellation, 64 MB and
  1 million triangles per imported model.

Sets are plain JSON. Fields that are missing take their defaults, so older
or hand-written files load; unknown fields are ignored. A minimal set:

```json
{
  "objects": [
    { "id": 1, "name": "Mia", "type": "character",
      "body": { "kind": "adult_female" },
      "pose": { "name": "my pose", "joints": { "head": { "y": 10 } }, "right_hand": "point" },
      "face": "happy",
      "transform": { "position": [0, 0, 0], "rotation": { "yaw": 20, "pitch": 0, "roll": 0 } } },
    { "id": 2, "name": "Table", "type": "prop", "source": "builtin", "kind": "table",
      "transform": { "position": [0, 0, 1.2] } },
    { "id": 3, "type": "light", "kind": "key", "intensity": 0.9,
      "transform": { "rotation": { "yaw": 35, "pitch": -50, "roll": 0 } } }
  ],
  "camera": { "position": [0, 1.6, 5], "yaw": 180, "focal_length_mm": 35 }
}
```

## Mannequins

Four body types: **adult male**, **adult female**, **adult** (neutral) and
**child**. Sliders:

| Slider | Range | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Height | 0.5–2.5 m | Overall height (default 1.78 / 1.65 / 1.72 / 1.20 m) |
| Build | 0–1 | Slim to heavy: limb and torso thickness |
| Head size | 0.7–1.5 | Head height multiplier (children already have larger heads) |
| Leg length | 0.8–1.2 | Longer legs, shorter torso; height stays the same |
| Arm length | 0.8–1.2 | Arm length |
| Shoulder width, hip width | 0.7–1.4 | Shoulder and hip spacing |

The figure is built from ellipsoids and tapered capsules attached rigidly to
a 31-bone skeleton: hips, spine, chest, neck, head; per side a shoulder,
upper arm, lower arm, hand, thumb (2 joints), index finger (2 joints), the
other fingers as one group (2 joints), upper leg, lower leg and foot. A small
nose shows which way the head faces.

With **ground lock** (on by default) the figure is lifted or lowered so its
lowest point rests on the ground, whatever the pose.

## Posing

### Joint rotations

Each joint stores three angles in degrees. Every bone's rest frame lines up
with the character (X = its left, Y = up, Z = forward), so the numbers read
the same on every bone:

| Bone | `x` | `y` | `z` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spine, chest, neck, head | + bends forward | + turns left | + leans right |
| Left upper arm or leg | − swings forward | twist | + raises outward |
| Left lower arm | − bends the elbow | twist | — |
| Lower leg | + bends the knee | — | — |
| Left fingers | — | — | − curls |

Right-side bones mirror the left: their `y` and `z` signs flip. Angles are
applied as twist first, then side swing, then front/back swing. Every joint
has limits (an elbow only bends one way, the head turns at most about 95°
with the neck); values outside are clamped.

### Poses, hand shapes and faces

A pose is data: joint angles (bones not listed stay at rest), a hips offset in
units of figure height (so one pose fits a child and an adult) and a hand
shape per hand. Poses can be **mirrored**, **blended** (joints interpolate;
hand shapes switch at the midpoint) and saved as custom poses in JSON.

Built-in poses: stand, relaxed stand, walk, run, sit, sit on floor, kneel,
crouch, point, wave, reach, arms crossed, hands on hips, fight stance, fall,
lie down, phone call, carry.

Hand shapes: open, relaxed, fist, point, grip.

Faces are drawn as lines on the head in the toon, clay and outline styles:
neutral, happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared, or blank.

### IK and look-at

- **Two-bone IK** moves a wrist or ankle to a target in the world. Elbows and
  knees are true hinges and bend toward a pole direction (elbows back and
  out, knees forward by default); when the preferred bend would break a
  shoulder or hip limit, the closest bend that respects it is used. Feet keep
  their orientation, so they stay flat.
- **Look-at** turns the neck and head toward a point; the neck takes about
  40% of the turn.
- IK targets and look-at live on the character until **baked** into the pose.

## Props

Built-in props are sized by width × height × depth in metres and sit on the
ground with their front toward +Z:

| Prop | Default size (m) | Options |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Box, cylinder, sphere | 0.5 × 0.5 × 0.5 (cylinder 1 m tall) | |
| Wall | 4 × 2.6 × 0.15 | |
| Floor | 6 × 0.02 × 6 | |
| Door | 0.9 × 2.1 × 0.12 | Opening angle |
| Window frame | 1.2 × 1.2 × 0.1 | |
| Table | 1.4 × 0.75 × 0.8 | Round top |
| Chair | 0.45 × 0.9 × 0.45 | |
| Bed | 1.6 × 0.9 × 2.1 | |
| Sofa | 2 × 0.85 × 0.9 | |
| Car | 1.8 × 1.45 × 4.5 | |
| Tree | 3 × 6 × 3 | |
| Lamp post | 0.4 × 4.5 × 0.4 | |
| Stairs | 1 × 1.44 × 2.4 | Number of steps |

### Imported models

- **glTF 2.0** (`.gltf` with embedded or neighbouring buffers, and `.glb`):
  triangle meshes, node transforms, base colours, double-sided materials,
  **base-colour textures**, vertex colours (`COLOR_0`) and **skins**. A
  skinned model can be posed by joint name, with the same three-angle joint
  rotations as mannequins.
- **Textures:** a material's `baseColorTexture` is read with its texture
  coordinate set (`TEXCOORD_0`, or the one it names) and its sampler's wrap
  modes (repeat, mirrored repeat, clamp). Images may be PNG or JPEG, stored
  in a buffer view, a data URI or (for `.gltf` files on disk) a neighbouring
  file. The texture, sampled with bilinear filtering, multiplies the base
  colour factor and any vertex colours; toon renders show it in colour and
  clay renders in grey. Images larger than 4096 pixels on a side, or more
  than 64 MB of decoded texture per model, are refused, as are damaged
  images and other formats (WebP, KTX2). A primitive without texture
  coordinates ignores its texture. Files without textures look as before.
- **OBJ:** vertices, normals and faces of any size; each `o`/`g` group becomes
  a named part.
- Not supported: textures other than the base colour (normal, metallic,
  emissive maps), texture transforms, animations, morph targets, sparse
  accessors and compressed meshes (Draco, meshopt). A file that needs them is refused with
  a message. USDZ and FBX are not supported.
- Buffers referenced by a `.gltf` must sit in the same folder or below it.
- A set that names a model which is not available shows a placeholder box.

## Cameras and lenses

- The camera has a position, yaw, pitch and roll, a **focal length** in mm
  and a **film back** (sensor size; Super 35, 24.89 × 18.66 mm, by default;
  full frame and Super 16 are presets).
- The film back's width sets the horizontal field of view:
  `hfov = 2 · atan(width / (2 · focal length))`. A 35 mm lens on Super 35
  sees about 39° across; 50 mm on full frame about 40°. The board's aspect
  ratio sets the vertical extent.
- Common primes for pickers: 14, 18, 24, 35, 50, 65, 85, 100 and 135 mm.
- **Top, side and front views** are orthographic cameras fitted to the set,
  for positioning objects.

### Shot sizes

Framing a shot keeps the lens and moves the camera so the subject fills the
frame from the top of the head (plus 12% headroom) to the size's cut line:

| Size | Shows |
| --- | --- |
| ECU — extreme close-up | The eyes, centred |
| CU — close-up | Head and the top of the shoulders |
| MCU — medium close-up | Head to mid-chest |
| MS — medium shot | Head to the waist |
| MWS — medium wide (cowboy) | Head to the knees |
| WS — wide shot | The whole figure |
| EWS — extreme wide shot | The figure small in the set |

Cut lines follow the posed skeleton, so a seated or crouching character is
framed by its own body. Props are framed by their bounding size. A shot can
also frame one body part (a hand, the face or a foot), or a whole group of
characters.

### Angles and sides

- **Angles:** eye level, low, high, bird's eye, worm's eye, dutch (rolled),
  over the shoulder (behind a second character's shoulder toward the
  subject) and two-shot (both characters, seen side on).
- **Sides**, relative to where the subject faces: front, three-quarter left
  and right, left and right profile, behind left and right, and behind.

### Shot Explorer

Given a subject, the Shot Explorer proposes varied setups, mixing size, angle,
side and a lens suited to the size (from 18 mm for extreme wide to 100 mm for
extreme close-ups), each with a name such as "Low-angle close-up, three-quarter
left". Over-the-shoulder and two-shots are included when there is another
character. The list is the same every time for the same set.

## Rendering

The renderer runs on the CPU and draws any size, with a transparent
background if wanted.

| Style | Look |
| --- | --- |
| Toon | Object colours in 2 or 3 light bands with contour lines |
| Clay | Smoothly shaded grey with contour lines |
| Outline | Lines only on white, for tracing |
| Silhouette | Solid black shapes |

- Contour lines come from object edges, depth jumps and creases (normal
  changes above 40° by default). Line width, colour and crease angle are
  adjustable; a selected object's lines can be drawn in a highlight colour.
- Lighting: key, fill and rim lights are directional and shine along their
  forward direction; ambient light comes from the environment.
- **Shadows** (Toon and Clay, on by default; the render option `shadows`
  turns them off): key lights cast shadows from characters and props onto
  the ground and onto each other. Each object has a **casts shadows**
  switch (`casts_shadows` in the set's JSON, on unless set to `false`); on a
  key light it switches that light's shadows, on a character or prop
  whether it casts one. Fill and rim lights never cast shadows, and at most
  the two strongest key lights do. In Toon, a shadowed surface takes the
  lowest light band and shadowed ground the same darkening; in Clay the
  blocked light's share is removed. Shadows come from a shadow map seen
  from the light, fitted to the objects that cast shadows (512–2048 texels
  a side, following the output size) and filtered at the edges; Outline and
  Silhouette have no shadows.
- The ground, a grid that fades with distance and a horizon line are drawn
  behind the set.
- Supersampling (2–4×) smooths edges for snapshots.
- Output is identical on every run and thread count.

A three-character, ten-prop set (about 19,000 triangles) is prepared and
rendered at 960 × 540 in roughly 25–30 ms without shadows and 45–50 ms with
them on four cores in release builds.

## Describe a shot

Type a shot in words and Emulsion builds the set, poses the characters and
frames the camera. Examples:

- "low-angle close-up of two people at a table"
- "wide shot of a woman running down a street"
- "over-the-shoulder of Mia talking to Tom"
- "bird's eye view of a car"
- "extreme close up of a hand"
- "high angle wide shot of a family of three in a park"
- "dutch angle cowboy shot of two angry men fighting, 85mm"

The parser recognizes these kinds of words (the longest phrase wins, so
"extreme close up" beats "close up"):

| Kind | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| Shot size | extreme close up, ECU, close-up, medium close up, medium shot, cowboy shot, wide shot, full shot, establishing shot |
| Angle | eye level, low angle, high angle, bird's eye, overhead, worm's eye, dutch, over the shoulder, two shot |
| Side | from behind, profile, three quarter, front view |
| Lens | 35mm, 85 mm, wide angle, telephoto, fisheye |
| People | a man, two women, three people, a child, kids, a couple, a family (of four) |
| Names | any capitalized unknown word: "Mia", "Tom" |
| Actions | running, walking, sitting, kneeling, crouching, pointing, waving, reaching, fighting, falling, lying down, on the phone, carrying, arms crossed, hands on hips, talking |
| Moods | happy, sad, angry, surprised, scared (and synonyms) |
| Props | table, chair, car, tree, door, window, bed, sofa, stairs, lamp post, box, wall |
| Places | street, room, office, kitchen, park, forest |
| Body parts | hand, face, eyes, foot |
| Relations | talking to, facing, looking at, on the floor |

How the set is built:

- People sit at a table on chairs, facing each other across it.
- Two people talking to each other face each other; without an angle they get
  a two-shot.
- Other groups stand in a row and are framed together in wide and medium shots.
- A street adds a road, building walls and lamp posts; a room adds a floor and
  walls; a park adds trees.
- When no person is mentioned, the first prop is the subject ("a car").
- Words the parser does not know are listed, so the assistant can refine the
  set through MCP.

## For integrators

The `emulsion-scene` crate has no UI. The main entry points:

- `Scene` with gizmo-friendly setters: `translate`, `rotate_about`,
  `set_scale`, `set_joint`, `set_ik_target`, `bake_pose`; `validate`,
  `normalize`, `to_json` and `from_json`.
- `prepare(scene, assets)` returns a `PreparedScene`; keep it while only the
  camera changes.
- `render(prepared, camera, w, h, options)` and the one-call
  `render_to_rgba(scene, assets, w, h, style)` return straight-alpha RGBA8.
- `pick` (object and bone or joint under a pixel), `pick_joint` and
  `joint_handles` (posing handles), `project_point` (attaching 2D layers to
  models), `attachment_matrix` and `SurfaceFrame` (layers on model
  surfaces).
- `frame_shot`, `explore_shots`, `text_to_shot` and `parse_shot`.
- `import_file` and `import_bytes`, with models kept in an `AssetLibrary`.
